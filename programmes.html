Programmes

Five programmes, one standard of evidence.

Each programme is designed to be funded and reported on separately. All of them use the same data systems and the same commitment to community consent.

01

Wildlife protection & anti-trafficking support

Somaliland’s coast and borders are used to move wildlife illegally, especially live cheetah cubs bound for the Arabian Peninsula. We support the government agencies, task forces and communities who detect and respond to these crimes.

What we do

  • Technical and coordination support to the Somaliland Wildlife and Forest Crime Unit and member agencies
  • Training for enforcement, prosecution and judicial staff on wildlife crime evidence
  • Community reporting channels and informant networks in priority areas
  • Cross-border information sharing with regional partners

What partners can fund

  • Agency training programmes
  • Community reporting networks
  • Case-support and coordination costs
02

Confiscated wildlife tracking

After a seizure, the animal needs care and the case needs evidence. We keep records that follow each confiscated animal from the moment of seizure to its final outcome.

What we do

  • Standardised seizure and intake records
  • Chain-of-custody documentation to support prosecution
  • Welfare, placement and outcome tracking
  • Aggregated reporting for CITES and partner obligations

What partners can fund

  • Database configuration and training
  • Field data-collection kits
  • Annual seizure and outcome reporting
03

Community conservation

People who live alongside wildlife protect it best when they have a real stake in doing so. We use free, prior and informed consent (FPIC) and community-led conservancy governance, drawing on what was learned setting up the C1 Awdal conservancy.

What we do

  • FPIC village consultations, repeated over the life of each programme
  • Conservancy governance, by-laws and committee support
  • Human–wildlife conflict response and rangeland management
  • Livelihood initiatives tied to conservation outcomes

What partners can fund

  • New conservancy establishment
  • Community ranger and monitor stipends
  • Governance workshops and exchange visits
04

Youth & conservation education

Somaliland is a young country and many of its future conservationists are in school today. We work with schools, universities and community and religious leaders to build understanding of Somaliland’s wildlife and why it matters.

What we do

  • School conservation clubs and curriculum materials in Somali and English
  • University placements and field-research opportunities
  • Community awareness through local leaders and media
  • Wildlife-crime awareness at ports and markets

What partners can fund

  • School programme cohorts
  • Student field placements
  • Awareness campaigns
05

Conservation data & donor reporting

The Fund does not report a figure it cannot evidence. Field surveys, monitoring and programme activity feed dashboards and reports that partners can inspect.

What we do

  • Species, habitat and socio-economic field surveys
  • Monitoring & evaluation frameworks agreed with each funder
  • Partner dashboards and quarterly reporting
  • Grant proposal development for local partners and agencies

What partners can fund

  • Baseline surveys
  • M&E frameworks and dashboards
  • Proposal development support
Where we work

Starting in the west, growing across Somaliland.

Our first work builds on the relationships and conservancy framework already in place in Awdal. We then plan to expand along trafficking routes and into other regions under pressure.

  • Awdal
  • Salal
  • Maroodi Jeex (Hargeisa)
  • Sahil (Berbera coast)
  • Wider Horn of Africa

Establishment phase. The Fund is currently preparing its first donor proposals and partner agreements. Programme scope in each region is agreed with communities, government and funders before work begins.
